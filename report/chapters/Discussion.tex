\chapter{Discussion}
\label{chap:discussion}

Chapter \ref{chapter:evaluation} examined whether the proposed LGVI--SDP framework 
preserves the geometric structure
of the modeled rigid-body systems,
produces certifiable open-loop trajectories,
and if feasible trajectories can be generated for not tight relaxations inside a closed loop implementation.
The results provide a qualified answer to the research question in Section \ref{sec:introduction_problem_statement}:
the framework is suitable for lagrangian variational integrators of single- and multibody systems
and can certify particular discrete trajectory-optimization instances.
Still tightness, feasible extraction,
and computational cost depend strongly on the considered dynamics and remain challenging.
In the following, the main findings are discussed and set into relation to the state of the art
Finally, potential applications are elaborated.

\section{Discussion of main Findings}
\label{sec:discussion_of_main_findings}
The simulation results in Section \ref{sec:numerical_simulation_results} 
establish that the derived discrete models of the 
3D pendulum on $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ and the underactuated Acrobot $(\mathrm{SO}(2) \times \mathbb{R}^2)^2$
represent the intended dynamics, while consiting enegery conservation properties over a long  time horizon.
For the unforced 3D pendulum,
Figure \ref{fig:simulation_results_3d_pendulum} shows that the LGVI remains on $\mathrm{SO}(3)$,
preserves the momentum
and exhibits bounded long-term energy error.
In comarison, a standard RK4 integrator has errors in all three quantities,
whereas the projected RK4 restores the rotation constraint but accelerates energy drifting.
The constrained Acrobot results in Figures \ref{fig:simulation_results_acrobot_unforced} 
and \ref{fig:simulation_results_acrobot_forced} extend this observation to a multibody system,
where both link rotations remain on $\mathrm{SO}(2)$,
the holonomic link constraints are satisfied,
and the unforced energy error remains bounded.
These result demonstrate that the derived discrete lagrangian variational 
integrators of the 3D-pendulum \eqref{eq:forced_3D_pendulum_EL} and the 
Acrobot \eqref{eq:acrobot_full_del_shifted} preserve the relevant configuration 
and constraint geometry over the simulated horizon.


\paragraph{Single-body optimization}
The 3D pendulum demonstrates the complete method for a single rigid body.
Its LGVI can be written as the quadratic POP in \eqref{eq:controlled_3d_pendulum_pop},
but a dense second-order relaxation would already require a $91{,}378\times91{,}378$ 
moment matrix for $N=20$.
The minimum-degree chordal extension generated by SPOT in Section \ref{subsec:sparse_relaxations} 
reduces the largest matrix to $325\times325$,
compared with $820\times820$ for the complete temporal clique.
Therfore, using Moment-SOS relaxation for converting an nonconvex 
algebraic LGVI formulation to a convex optimization problem is possible.
Still the MD sparsity construction is required for computational application.

For all five targets $\theta_{x,des}=\{0^\circ, 45^\circ, 90^\circ, 135^\circ, 180^\circ\}$,
Table \ref{tab:pendulum_3d_sdp_statistics} reports a rank metric below $7.3\times10^{-8}$,
a manifold violation below $4.6\times10^{-8}$,
and an suboptimality gap below $1.8\times10^{-6}$.
Additionally, the solution extractions remain feasible.
Therefore, these values indicate that the second-order relaxations are numerically tight and
certify global optimality for the considered discrete POP.
Figure \ref{fig:optimization_results_x180_md} illustrates the $\theta_{x,des}=180^\circ$ case,
where the extracted trajectory reaches the desired attitude while remaining on $\mathrm{SO}(3)$.
Consequently, the single-body problem of an 3D-Pendulum on $SO(3)$ is globally certifiable 
using discrete LGVI dynamics and sparse SDP relaxations.

Still, it has to be noted that the certified solutions do not necessarily 
reach the desired states after exactly $N$ steps, while still being certifiable. For example,
the $\theta_{x,des}=90^\circ$ case reaches a terminal state of $\theta_{x,final}\approx70.5^\circ$,
since the implemented target is not a hard constraint but a cost inside the objective function.
Therefore, the certified solution is optimal for the discrete objective, 
but not for the exact terminal arrival.

\paragraph{Multibody formulation and sparsity}
The Acrobot extends the framework to a constrained and underactuated multibody system.
Its maximal-coordinate LGVI represents both links separately
and imposes the joints through holonomic constraints and multipliers.
This enables a quadratic polynomial structure.
Moreover, the reduction introduced in Section \ref{sec:discrete_motion_planning_acrobot} eliminates 
the position variables and expresses the velocities through rotation variables.
Thereby, the size of the decision vector, which has to be optimized, 
decreases from $17N+3$ to $13N-1$ variables while preserving quadratic polynomial dynamics.
It therefore reduces the SDP without introducing densely coupled dynamical 
representations when using with minimal coordinates \cite{BrudigamManchester2021LQR}.

Nevertheless, even after the reduction,
the dense second-order moment matrix has the dimension $304{,}590\times304{,}590$ for $N=60$.
The complete SELF chain \eqref{eq:acrobot_full_self_initial_clique}-
\eqref{eq:acrobot_full_self_interior_cliques} replaces the dense 
matrix with seperated blocks of the size $351\times351$ and $253\times253$.
The multibody-specific construction in \eqref{eq:acrobot_hybrid_self_cliques} 
keeps the complete cliques at $k=1,2$,
but separates later dynamics and kinematics into $171\times171$ and $91\times91$ matrices.
The results of the SDP relaxation, shown in the Tables \ref{tab:acrobot_sdp_statistics_full} and
\ref{tab:acrobot_sdp_statistics_hybrid},
identifies similar relaxation metrics for both structures,
while the specialized cliques reduce the MOSEK runtime by a factor of approximately three to five.
In consequence, the identification of problem-specific sparsity is necessary 
for solving multi-body systems using semidefinite programming, in order to get 
fast and numerically reliable results \cite{Kang_2025Global}. 

However, it is important to note that multibody-specific sparsity do not guarantee tight relaxations 
rather than using chain-like sparsity. Therefore, before applying the proposed framework to new multibody systems, 
it is necessary to evaluate the tightness of the relaxation for the specific problem.

\paragraph{Open-loop Acrobot optimization}
The open-loop SDP evaluation in Section\ref{subsec:acrobot_optimization_results} 
separates easy from difficult Acrobot problems.
For the targets $\theta_{A,des}=0^\circ$ and $\theta_{A,des}=45^\circ$,
Tables \ref{tab:acrobot_sdp_statistics_full} and \ref{tab:acrobot_sdp_statistics_hybrid} report 
rank-one moment matrices, feasible extractions, and negligible suboptimality gaps.
Therefore, in these cases, the solution are certified.
However, similar to the 3D-Pendulum,the certified $45^\circ$ solution retains a terminal 
error of approximately $55^\circ$, because the target is only weighted in the objective.

For the targets $\theta_{A,des}=90^\circ$, $\theta_{A,des}=135^\circ$, and $\theta_{A,des}=180^\circ$,
the maximum rank increase to approximately $0.35$-$0.66$,
while the extracted rotations have manifold violations around $1.0$-$1.4$.
The first moments therefore do not define feasible $\mathrm{SO}(2)$ trajectories
and no certificate is obtained. The results indicate that the additional complexity of the multibody
underactuated system, increases the difficulty of the convex optimization problem.

For the runtime, the optimization using specialized multimody cliques \eqref{eq:acrobot_hybrid_self_cliques} 
require approximately $15$-$31$ minutes,
compared with $45$-$113$ minutes for the chain-like cliques \eqref{eq:acrobot_full_self_initial_clique}-
\eqref{eq:acrobot_full_self_interior_cliques}.
Therefore, it demonstrates the size of the moment matrices is the driving factor for the runtime of the SDP solver.
Still in both cases, they remain too slow for online control.
A lower relaxation order would reduce matrix sizes but weaken the tightness of the relaxation.
Moreover, instead of using the second order interior-point solver MOSEK \cite{MOSEK}, 
a first-order SDP solver, for example CuADMM \cite{Kang_2026Fast}, could accelerate the same relaxation
at the possible cost of less accurate certified solutions. Additionally, in this thesis, only 
second order $\kappa = 2$ relaxations are considered, as \cite{Sangli2025}
states that second order relaxations are sufficient for most of the considered problems. In general, 
higher order relaxations can be used to tighten the relaxation \eqref{eq:convergence_of_moment_sos_hierarchy}, 
but at the cost of increased computational complexity.

\paragraph{Closed-loop Acrobot stabilization}
Tables~6.4 and~6.5 show that $\delta_1$ is smaller than the worst-horizon metric $\delta_{\max}$.
This motivates the receding-horizon implementation in Section~5.4.2:
only the first control from Equation~(5.66) is applied,
the remaining non-tight prediction is discarded,
and the SDP is solved again from the simulated state.
Replanning introduces feedback,
but $\delta_1<\delta_{\max}$ is not a rank-one condition and does not restore certifiability.

Figure~6.6 shows that this procedure stabilizes the symmetric $5^\circ$ state
after approximately $11.9\,\mathrm{s}$ with $u_{\max}=5\,\mathrm{N\,m}$.
The simulated state remains on $\mathrm{SO}(2)$
although the predicted blocks are not rank one throughout the maneuver.
This demonstrates numerical closed-loop feasibility for one state,
not a globally optimal control sequence,
a certified region of attraction,
or robustness.
The additional failures are also consistent with limited control authority at this bound.

With $u_{\max}=15\,\mathrm{N\,m}$,
Figure~6.7 shows that both matched-time Cases~I and~II stabilize $94$ of $100$ initial configurations.
Thus, consistently refining all intervals from $0.1\,\mathrm{s}$ to $0.05\,\mathrm{s}$
does not change the aggregate success rate.
The different failed cells nevertheless reveal numerical sensitivity
and prevent interpreting the result as a discretization-convergence proof.

Only $34$ configurations stabilize in the mismatched Case~III.
Here, a control predicted for $0.1\,\mathrm{s}$ is applied for $0.025\,\mathrm{s}$
and the coarse incoming rotation is reconstructed from the fine history.
Figure~6.8 shows a more oscillatory common success requiring approximately $16\,\mathrm{s}$
instead of $3.1\,\mathrm{s}$ in Case~I.
The results therefore indicate reduced reliability and efficiency under mismatched time scales.
Because several sampling ratios and the history reconstruction change together,
no single cause is isolated.
An infeasible SDP iteration also indicates controller failure,
not that the plant state is fundamentally unstabilizable.

For the successful $(80^\circ,80^\circ)$ example,
Cases~I--III require approximately $3.1\,\mathrm{h}$,
$27.3\,\mathrm{h}$,
and $17.8\,\mathrm{h}$.
These illustrative runtimes depend on $\Delta t_{\mathrm{SDP}}$ and $N_{\mathrm{SDP}}$,
which determine each SDP size,
and on the number of replanning iterations.
A matched $0.0025\,\mathrm{s}$ case would require $N=800$
and was computationally intractable.
The closed-loop implementation is therefore an offline feasibility study rather than a real-time controller.

\section{Comparison to the State of the Art}
\label{sec:comparison_state_of_the_art}
The preservation properties of both LGVIs agree with the theory
in \cite{Marsden2001,Lee2008}
and the maximal-coordinate multibody formulations
in \cite{BrudigamManchester2021VariationalIntegrators,BrudigamManchester2021LQR}.
The contribution is therefore not a new conservation theorem,
but the integration of reduced structure-preserving models into polynomial trajectory optimization.

The closest framework is \cite{Sangli2025}.
The certified pendulum and easy Acrobot cases confirm its principal benefit,
whereas the difficult cases confirm that tightness is problem-dependent.
While \cite{Sangli2025} refines non-tight extractions with NLP,
this thesis repeatedly applies only the first SDP control.
NLP refinement seeks a feasible full-horizon trajectory;
replanning seeks numerical closed-loop stabilization.
Neither provides a global certificate when the relaxation remains non-tight.

The importance of sparsity and solver choice is consistent
with \cite{Waki2006Sparse,Lasserre2006Sparse,Kang_2026Fast,Kang_2025Global}.
GPU and first-order solvers may make larger instances accessible,
but faster solution does not guarantee tighter moment matrices.
Compared with the local methods reviewed in Section~1.2,
the present SDP approach is considerably slower
but supplies a global lower bound and rank diagnostics.
Its repeated-relaxation structure is related to SDP-based MPC such as \cite{Hartmann_2026SDP},
here evaluated for constrained underactuated Lie-group dynamics.

\section{Potential Applications}
\label{sec:potential_applications}
The most direct application is offline trajectory generation and assessment.
Tight cases provide certified benchmarks for evaluating local optimizers,
initializing an NLP,
or generating references for faster tracking controllers.
The terminal errors also show that objective design remains important:
a globally optimal solution of a soft tracking cost may still be practically unsatisfactory.

For non-tight cases,
the lower bounds, diagnostics,
and first controls remain useful even without a feasible open-loop extraction.
Repeated offline SDP--MPC simulations could generate feasible state--control demonstrations
for imitation learning or offline reinforcement learning.
A learned policy could then approximate the demonstrated behavior at substantially lower online cost.

Such demonstrations are feasible only for the implemented numerical model
and are not globally optimal labels when the relaxation is non-tight.
Solver failures can also bias the data toward easier regions.
Solver status, residuals, rank metrics,
objective bounds,
stabilization time,
and discretization should therefore accompany each trajectory.
The results support offline references and data generation,
but do not yet establish real-time control,
robustness under uncertainty,
contact-rich planning,
or hardware performance.
